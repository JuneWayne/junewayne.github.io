Effective Data Curation for Robust Prompt Injection Defense Fine-Tuning

Cisco Foundation AI · Virginia AI Safety Initiative

Existing approaches include fine-tuning LLMs as judges, training hidden-state probes, and training lightweight classifiers to distinguish prompt injections from benign inputs. I build on ODILE (Özdinçer et al., 2026), a method that uses LoRA to fine-tune a small language model with a training objective that separates injection and safe-prompt representations. I examine which curated datasets of injection and safe prompts produce the best fine-tuned judge model. Using paired safe and injection prompts from the FIPI (Fine-grained Indirect Prompt Injection) dataset (Zhong et al., 2026), this project compares data selected using different trained probes, Jlens, random selection, diversity clustering, and model failure. I evaluate whether each selection method reduces injection-following behavior while preserving legitimate task completion.

Interpreting 3D Autoregressive Transformers

Spatial AI Lab

Studying how attention heads in autoregressive mesh transformers relate to the geometric properties of triangles, with comparisons across four 3D generation model families.

Details

The current analysis examines MeshGPT, MeshXL, DeepMesh, and MeshMosaic using 500 ShapeNet meshes across five object categories. It compares attention-head preferences for triangle area, aspect ratio, and orientation.

Tracking Semantic Evolution of Data Science Roles

Active Learning Lab

Details

PARC: Physics-Aware Recurrent Convolutional Neural Network

Visual Intelligence Lab

Adapted PARCv2 to model advection and diffusion in 2D shear flow, predicting tracer, pressure, and velocity fields. Experiments compared Euler and RK4 integration and reduced rollout MSE from approximately 0.12 to 0.0228 on a training sample.

Details

Research Assistant · Charlottesville, Virginia · April – September 2025

Kept Reynolds and Schmidt parameter fields constant in the differentiator while allowing the integrator to evolve velocity fields. Explored Schmidt-based data splits and compared snapshot predictions with autoregressive rollouts.

Snapshot predictions were stronger than longer rollouts. Euler trained faster, while RK4 produced steadier loss curves but still accumulated rollout errors. Broader test-set validation and physics-based evaluation were identified as next steps.

The animation shows RK4 snapshot predictions of the tracer field beside ground truth on a test sample (slide 40). The full presentation contains all 83 slides, including earlier experiments and their limitations.

  • Designed and trained a Physics-Aware Recurrent Convolutional (PARCv2) neural network in PyTorch to predict advection and diffusion in 2D shear-flow fluid dynamics systems.
  • Reduced training time from over 8 hours to 6.75 minutes per full-trajectory training sample through spatial downsampling, temporal skipping, and training-pipeline changes, as reported in the presentation.
  • Reduced rollout inference MSE on a training sample from approximately 0.12 to 0.0228 (about 81%); longer rollouts remained sensitive to compounding errors.
  • Designed differentiator and integrator components that keep constant variables at zero time derivatives while evolving velocity fields in the model state, enabling PARC to learn 2D shear-flow advection and diffusion.
